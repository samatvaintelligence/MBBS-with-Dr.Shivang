## 1. Brand Voice & Language

### Writing style

All creative copy is written in **English** — clear, direct, empathetic. Not Hinglish.

Dr. Shivang speaks in **first person**. He is not a brand, a company, or a consultancy. He is one person who dropped NEET three times, went to Russia, graduated, and is now an intern at G.R.M.C. Gwalior. Every piece of copy should sound like a real person talking to a younger version of himself.

**Tone keywords:** honest, calm, personal, anti-consultant, zero-pressure

### Headline rules

- ALL CAPS for every headline on visual creatives
- Short, punchy, under 12 words
- End with a period or question mark for emphasis
- Lead with the reader's pain, not the product

**Headline formulas:**

| Formula | Example |
|---------|---------|
| POV: [pain point] | POV: NEET DIDN'T GO AS PLANNED... BUT YOU DIDN'T GIVE UP. |
| [Number] + [bold claim] | AFTER 3 PAINFUL YEARS, I STOPPED CHASING CUTOFFS. |
| [Myth framing] | WHAT YOU'VE GOT WRONG ABOUT M.B.B.S. IN RUSSIA |
| [Direct question] | NEET-UG CUTOFF TOO HIGH? |
| [Bold statement] | I DROPPED NEET-UG 3 TIMES. BUT I STILL BECAME A DOCTOR. |
| [Money hook] | MBBS ABROAD. NO DONATION. NMC APPROVED. ENGLISH-MEDIUM. |

### Caption structure

Every caption follows this skeleton:

```

[Personal connection — "I've been there" or "I know how it feels"]

[The shift — "But there is another path" / "Here's the truth"]
```

- `DM me "FEES"` — cost comparison posts
- `DM me "PARENTS"` — parent-facing content

Never say "link in bio" as the primary CTA. The DM trigger is warmer and lower friction.

### Words and phrases

**Use freely:**
- "I've been exactly in that place"
- "This is not the end"
- "You deserve to hear the truth"
- "No pressure, no consultancy fee"
- "Real doctor, not a consultant"
- "Step by step"
- "Your dream is still possible"

**Never use:**
- "We" / "our team" / "our mentors" (it's one person)

- "Best university" / "Top-ranked" (unverifiable)
- "100% guarantee" / "Guaranteed admission"
- "Limited time offer" (infomercial energy)
- USD or dollar amounts (audience thinks in lakhs/INR)

## 2. Color Palette

### Design rationale

The palette is built from **split-complementary harmony** on the color wheel — the most versatile scheme for brands that need to project trust AND warmth simultaneously.

- **Base hue: 215°** (navy blue) — trust, authority, medicine
- **Split complement 1: 42°** (amber) — aspiration, achievement, premium
- **Split complement 2: 8°** (coral) — warmth, empathy, human emotion

This gives natural visual tension (grabs attention on a scroll) without the chaos of arbitrary color choices.

### The 60-30-10 rule

Every slide follows this distribution or the design falls apart:

| Share | Role | Colors |
|-------|------|--------|
| **60%** | Dominant — sets the mood, is the canvas | Midnight, Navy, or photo with overlay |
| **30%** | Secondary — carries the readable content | Ivory, White |
| **10%** | Accent — draws the eye to what matters | Amber (primary) or Coral (emotional) |

If you're using accent color on more than 10% of the slide area, you're doing it wrong. Accent colors work because they're rare. The moment you flood a slide with gold, it stops being gold — it becomes wallpaper.

### Token table

| Token | Hex | HSL | Role |
|-------|-----|-----|------|
| **Midnight** | `#0A1628` | 215° / 66% / 10% | Primary dark background. Never use pure #000000 — it has no depth. Midnight has a blue undertone that subconsciously reads as "medical, professional." |
| **Navy** | `#1E3A5F` | 212° / 52% / 25% | Secondary background for cards, CTA strips, info panels. Lighter than Midnight for layering depth. |
| **Slate** | `#64748B` | 215° / 16% / 47% | De-emphasized text, divider lines, timestamps, secondary info. Never for headlines. |
| **Ivory** | `#F7F5F0` | 43° / 30% / 95% | Primary body text on dark backgrounds. Warmer than pure white — reduces eye strain on mobile OLED screens and reads as more premium. |
| **White** | `#FFFFFF` | 0° / 0% / 100% | Headlines on dark backgrounds when you need maximum contrast. Reserve for the single most important text element per slide. |
| **Amber** | `#E8A317` | 42° / 85% / 50% | Primary accent. CTAs, emphasized words in headlines, pill badges, buttons. This is not generic yellow — it's the hue of real gold, shifted warm to avoid "caution tape" energy. |
| **Coral** | `#D94F3D` | 8° / 66% / 55% | Emotional accent. Used ONLY for the single pain-point word per slide — "NEET", "3 TIMES", "DROPPED". One word, not a sentence. Two accent colors on one slide = visual noise. |
| **Sage** | `#2D9F6F` | 155° / 56% / 40% | Checkmarks, "approved" labels, positive indicators. This is the green of surgical scrubs and hospital signage — muted, professional, trustworthy. Not the radioactive green of gaming UIs. |
| **Photo Overlay** | `rgba(10, 22, 40, 0.55)` | — | Tinted overlay on full-bleed photos. Uses Midnight as the tint color (not neutral black) so the overlay adds depth rather than just dimming the image. |

### Pairing rules

**Allowed accent combinations per slide:**
- Amber only (most slides — informational, cost, CTA)
- Coral only (emotional/personal story slides)
- **Never Amber + Coral on the same slide** — they are split complements and compete for the eye

**Background + text pairings:**

| Background | Headline | Body | Accent |
|------------|----------|------|--------|
| Midnight `#0A1628` | White `#FFF` | Ivory `#F7F5F0` | Amber `#E8A317` |
| Navy `#1E3A5F` | White `#FFF` | Ivory `#F7F5F0` | Amber `#E8A317` |
| Photo + overlay | White `#FFF` | Ivory `#F7F5F0` | Amber or Coral |
| Ivory `#F7F5F0` (rare) | Midnight `#0A1628` | Navy `#1E3A5F` | Amber `#E8A317` |

### Contrast ratios (verified)

| Pair | Ratio | WCAG | Pass? |
|------|-------|------|-------|
| White on Midnight | 17.4:1 | AAA | Yes |
| Ivory on Midnight | 15.2:1 | AAA | Yes |
| Amber on Midnight | 8.4:1 | AAA | Yes |
| Coral on Midnight | 5.2:1 | AA | Yes |
| Ivory on Navy | 9.1:1 | AAA | Yes |
| Amber on Navy | 5.1:1 | AA | Yes |
| Midnight on Ivory | 15.2:1 | AAA | Yes |
| Slate on Midnight | 3.8:1 | — | Fails for body text. Use Slate only for decorative/secondary elements, never for readable content. |

### Why these specific values

**Midnight `#0A1628` instead of black:** Pure black (#000000) next to white creates a "halation" effect on OLED screens — the white text appears to bleed at the edges. A 10% lightness dark with blue undertone eliminates this while reading as "black" to the viewer. Every luxury brand (Rolex, Chanel, Apple's dark mode) uses chromatic blacks, never true black.

**Ivory `#F7F5F0` instead of white:** On a 6-inch OLED screen viewed in a dimly lit room (how most Indian students browse Instagram), pure white (#FFF) body text on dark backgrounds is physically fatiguing. Ivory at 95% lightness with a warm tint reduces perceived brightness by ~15% without looking "off" or yellowed.

**Amber `#E8A317` instead of yellow:** Generic yellow (#FFD700, #FFFF00) is the most overused accent color on Indian Instagram. It codes as "discount sale" or "cricket highlight." Amber at hue 42° with reduced lightness reads as gold/premium — it's the difference between a gold medal and a highlighter pen.

**Coral `#D94F3D` instead of hot pink:** Hot pink (#FF1493) is high saturation at hue 328° — it's a cold color despite feeling "loud." Coral at hue 8° is genuinely warm. The eye processes it as "human" and "emotional" rather than "neon sign." It's the color of flushed skin, terracotta, warmth.

**Sage `#2D9F6F` instead of bright green:** Bright green (#2ECC71, #00FF00) triggers "notification" or "online status" pattern recognition. Sage is desaturated enough to read as "medicine" and "growth" without screaming for attention — exactly what a supporting color should do.

## 3. Typography

### Theory: contrast + kinship

The foundational rule of font pairing is **contrast in structure, kinship in quality**. Two fonts should differ visibly in their skeleton (condensed vs. proportional, geometric vs. humanist) but share the same level of craftsmanship and optical refinement. Two fonts from the same structural category look like a mistake. Two fonts at different quality levels look cheap.

### Font pairing

| Role | Font | Category | Why this font |
|------|------|----------|---------------|
| **Display** | **Oswald** | Condensed geometric sans | Tall, narrow letterforms command authority without shouting. Multiple weights (300-700) give flexibility that single-weight Bebas Neue cannot. ALL CAPS Oswald at Bold weight is as impactful as Impact/Bebas but with better kerning and a lighter optical weight that doesn't smother the slide. |
| **Body** | **Source Sans 3** | Humanist sans | Adobe's open-source workhorse. Humanist construction (letters based on pen strokes, not compass geometry) creates subconscious warmth — the typographic equivalent of Dr. Shivang's empathetic tone. Exceptional legibility at 42px on mobile screens. 6 weights for granular hierarchy. |

**Alternative (if tools don't support these):**
- Oswald alternative: Barlow Condensed (similar vertical proportion)
- Source Sans 3 alternative: Nunito Sans (slightly rounder, same warmth)
- **Avoid:** Poppins (too rounded/playful for medical authority), Inter (too neutral/tech), Roboto (too generic)

Both fonts are available on Google Fonts and in Canva.

### Modular scale

All type sizes are derived from a **Perfect Fourth ratio (1:1.333)**. This creates a natural visual rhythm — each size feels "right" relative to its neighbors because the ratio mirrors musical harmony.

Base size: **42px** (body text, optimized for readability on a 1080px canvas viewed on a 6-inch phone screen).

| Level | Size | Ratio | Font + Weight | Use |
|-------|------|-------|---------------|-----|
| **Display** | 133px | base × 1.333⁴ | Oswald Bold (700), ALL CAPS | Hook headlines — the one line that stops the scroll |
| **H1** | 100px | base × 1.333³ | Oswald SemiBold (600), ALL CAPS | Section headlines on info slides |
| **H2** | 75px | base × 1.333² | Oswald Medium (500), ALL CAPS or sentence | Sub-headlines, second line of a two-line hook |
| **H3** | 56px | base × 1.333¹ | Source Sans 3 Bold (700) | Card titles, pill badge labels, emphasized body |
| **Body** | 42px | base | Source Sans 3 Regular (400) | Paragraph text, checklist items, truth explanations |
| **Small** | 32px | base ÷ 1.333 | Source Sans 3 Regular (400) | Supporting text, disclaimers, longer paragraphs |
| **Caption** | 24px | base ÷ 1.333² | Source Sans 3 SemiBold (600), ALL CAPS | Watermark, CTA strip labels, fine print |

### Vertical rhythm

**Line heights:**

| Level | Line height | Why |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| Display / H1 | 1.0 | Tight leading on headlines creates a dense, powerful block of text. The letters SHOULD almost touch vertically — it communicates urgency and authority. |
| H2 | 1.1 | Slightly more air for two-line sub-heads |
| H3 | 1.2 | Bridge between headline density and body openness |
| Body | 1.5 | Standard readable leading. At 42px, this means 63px line height — enough air between lines for the eye to track back to the start without losing its place. |
| Small | 1.45 | Slightly tighter than body because the text is smaller and used in denser contexts |

**Letter spacing:**

| Level | Tracking | Why |
|-------|----------|-----|
| Display ALL CAPS | +4px | Condensed ALL CAPS letters crash into each other without tracking. +4px opens them to breathe without losing the condensed character. |
| H1 ALL CAPS | +3px | Slightly less than display since the letters are smaller |
| H2 | +1px | Minimal tracking, just enough to prevent tight pairs |
| Body | 0px | Never touch body letter spacing — the font designer already optimized it |
| Caption ALL CAPS | +6px | Small ALL CAPS text needs wide tracking or it becomes an illegible smear |

### Text rendering on photos

Text on photographic backgrounds requires one of two treatments. There is no third option — bare text on a photo is never acceptable.

**Option A: Full overlay** (preferred for hook slides)
- Apply `Photo Overlay` (`rgba(10, 22, 40, 0.55)`) over the entire photo
- Text sits directly on the overlay
- Headline in White, body in Ivory
- Best for: slides where the entire surface has text

**Option B: Localized shadow** (for slides with partial text)
- No full overlay — the photo is prominent
- Each text element gets: `text-shadow: 0 4px 12px rgba(10, 22, 40, 0.8)`
- Plus a subtle secondary shadow: `0 1px 3px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.9)` for hard legibility at the letter edge
- Best for: myth/truth slides, slides where photo context matters

**Never:** gradient overlays (amateur), colored tints over photos (destroys photo value), text with stroke/outline (90s web design).

## 4. Layout & Composition

### Canvas and grid

**Canvas:** 1080 x 1350 px (4:5 Instagram portrait).

**12-column grid:**

```
←45px→ ←66→ 18 ←66→ 18 ←66→ 18 ←66→ 18 ←66→ 18 ←66→ 18 ←66→ 18 ←66→ 18 ←66→ 18 ←66→ 18 ←66→ 18 ←66→ ←45px→
       col1     col2     col3     col4     col5     col6     col7     col8     col9     col10    col11    col12
```

- Column width: 66px
- Gutter: 18px
- Side margins: 45px
- Content width: 990px (12 columns + 11 gutters)

**Vertical baseline grid:** 24px. Every element's top edge snaps to a multiple of 24px from the top of the canvas. This creates invisible alignment that the eye perceives as "polished" without knowing why.

### Safe zones

```
┌──────────────────────────────┐
│  ░░░░░░ TOP: 72px ░░░░░░░░  │ ← IG username bar overlaps here
│                              │
│  SAFE CONTENT AREA           │
│  (990 x 1134 px)            │
│                              │
│                              │
│                              │
│  ░░ BOTTOM: 144px ░░░░░░░░  │ ← CTA strip (96px) + dots/margin (48px)
└──────────────────────────────┘
     ↑                    ↑
   45px                 45px
   margin               margin
```

- **Top safe zone:** 72px (3 baseline units) — Instagram overlays the username bar here
- **Bottom reserved:** 144px — 96px for CTA strip + 48px breathing room for carousel dots
- **Side margins:** 45px minimum — content in the outer 45px gets cropped on some devices
- **Safe content area:** 990 x 1134 px

### Composition: reading patterns

The eye does not scan a slide randomly. It follows predictable paths based on content type. Each template is designed around the correct reading pattern.

**Z-pattern** (image-heavy slides — hook slides, CTA slides):
```
1 ──────→ 2         The eye enters top-left, scans right,
          ╲          then diagonals to bottom-left,
           ╲         then scans right again.
3 ──────→ 4
```
Place: headline at position 1-2, visual/photo in the middle diagonal, CTA at position 3-4.

**F-pattern** (text-heavy slides — info slides, checklists):
```
1 ──────────→        Full scan across the top (headline)
2 ────→              Shorter scan (first list item)
│                    Then vertical scan down the left edge
3                    (scanning start of each item)
│
4
```
Place: headline full-width at top, list items left-aligned, most important item FIRST.

### Golden section for photo/text splits

When dividing a slide into a photo zone and a text zone, use the **golden ratio (1:1.618)** instead of 50/50.

- **Photo-dominant:** Photo gets 834px (61.8%), text gets 516px (38.2%)
- **Text-dominant:** Text gets 834px, photo gets 516px
- A 50/50 split looks indecisive. The golden section looks intentional.

### Negative space

The single biggest difference between amateur and professional design is **negative space**. Content should fill no more than **60-65%** of the slide area — the remaining 35-40% is breathing room.

Rules:
- Minimum 45px margins on all four sides (non-negotiable)
- Minimum 48px between headline and the next element
- Minimum 32px between distinct content blocks
- CTA buttons need 40px of clear space on all sides
- When in doubt, add more space, not more content

- Fill more than 65% of a slide with content
